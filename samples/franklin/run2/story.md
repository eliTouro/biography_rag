## Birth and Family

[D06-C0004][D01-C0021][D04-C0003][D07-C0001][D05-C0023] His father was from Ecton, Northamptonshire, England, had worked as a dyer before moving to New England, and then set up there as a tallow-chandler and soap-boiler. ## Boyhood and Schooling

As a child, Franklin had very little formal schooling, but he was already a good reader and speller. [D05-C0028] He learned a great deal on his own and was ahead of most boys his age. [D05-C0028] He was sent to grammar school at about age eight and later attended a school for writing and arithmetic. [D06-C0004][D01-C0022][D03-C0032][D07-C0001] He was taken from school to help his father in the candle-making business. [D06-C0004] He also read widely as a boy, loved books, and later said he had been reading everything he could lay his hands on at twelve years of age. ## Marriage, Children, and Friends

[D02-C0024][D03-C0097][D04-C0033] Their marriage is described as a good and faithful partnership, and they were said to have tried to make each other happy. ## Work, Travel, and Reputation

Franklin became known for his discoveries in electricity, and the sources describe him as eminent in science, public service, diplomacy, and literature. [D04-C0040][D01-C0006] He is also linked with the Franklin stove, the lightning-rod, and the discovery of the identity of lightning with electricity. [D02-C0042][D03-C0155][D04-C0040][D03-C0157][D03-C0158] He spent about eighteen months in London and also traveled into France, Holland, and Germany. [D06-C0030][D06-C0098] On his return to America, he set out for Havre, was at Rouen, and was at Portsmouth before sailing home. [D07-C0032] He later took a dangerous voyage to France on the Reprisal with his grandson, and English cruisers chased the ship several times. [D02-C0212] If he had been caught, he might have faced long imprisonment or even a trial for high treason. [D02-C0212] He was said to have had political enemies in Pennsylvania, especially the proprietary party, and Dr. Lee and Mr. Izard are named among them. [D02-C0358][D03-C0111] Some hostility to Franklin also showed itself in Congress, and Adams and others were irritated by the French people's view of him. [D03-C0280] He was fond of wordy contention when he was younger, though he later thought that disputatious turn was a very bad habit. [D03-C0035]

## Later Years and Death

Franklin lost a son to smallpox and regretted not having inoculated him. [D01-C0147] ⚠ He also said this was an example of a regretful decision. [D01-C0147] His greatest sorrow was connected to his son William adhering to the mother country, and the sources call this the loss of his only legitimate son and a source of profound mortification.
